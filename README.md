## User Research Case Study: African Language Learning App

### Problem

Many African parents living abroad may want their children to learn an African language and connect with their cultural heritage. However, there may be barriers such as screen-time concerns, privacy concerns, inappropriate content and cost.

### Research Questions

The research would explore:

- Why parents might choose an app instead of a teacher.
- Parents' concerns about children using digital devices.
- Whether parents would be willing to pay for the app.
- Features parents expect from a children's language-learning app.
- Whether children are interested in learning through an app.
- What would make the app valuable enough for parents to pay for.

### Key Findings

For this practice case study, a survey of 100 parents identified four major concerns:

- 65% were concerned about screen time.
- 50% were concerned about privacy.
- 30% were concerned about inappropriate content.
- 15% were concerned about cost.

### Recommendations

#### 1. Screen Time

Consider parental controls, usage reminders or other tools that help parents manage children's screen time.

#### 2. Privacy

Minimize the personal information collected during registration and clearly explain how children's and parents' data is handled and protected.

#### 3. Content Safety

Use appropriate content-review and moderation processes because the application is designed for children.

#### 4. Cost

Conduct further research into parents' willingness to pay and consider options such as free trials or affordable pricing tiers.

### Conclusion

The research suggests that increasing adoption is not only about marketing the application. Parents need to feel that the product is safe, useful, accessible and valuable for their children.# Open Source Community & User Research Project 
## Project Overview
This project explores how an open-source African-language learning application for children could attract and retain users, particularly children of African parents living abroad.
## Target Users
- African parents living abroad
- Children learning African languages
- Families interested in African culture and languages
## Community Growth Strategy
My initial approach would be to:
1. Learn and understand the application thoroughly.
2. Create simple videos demonstrating how the application works.
3. Share the content across social media platforms.
4. Build a small community of potential users.
5. Encourage users to try the application.
6. Collect feedback from parents and children.
7. Identify problems affecting user engagement.
8. Recommend improvements based on user feedback.
## User Experience Ideas
Children may respond positively to:
- Interactive activities
- Age-appropriate music
- Engaging colours and visuals
- Games and challenges
- Simple navigation
- Opportunities to explore independently
## Goal
The goal is to understand potential users, encourage adoption, collect meaningful feedback and identify ways an open-source project could improve its user experience and community engagement.
